Abstract

Connected-vehicle safety applications couple communication with physical driving: delayed, missing, or incorrect vehicle-to-everything (V2X) information can alter vehicle decisions and compromise safety. Testing such failures requires jointly specifying traffic interactions, communication conditions, fault timing, and application logic, creating a large and semantically complex scenario space that is difficult to construct manually. Natural language provides an intuitive interface for expressing these coupled test intents, but translating them into executable scenarios requires ensuring both physical feasibility and causal validity.

This paper presents LAST-V2X, an LLM-orchestrated framework that transforms natural-language V2X test intents into executable, communication-aware CARLA scenarios and adaptively refines them to expose causally verified failures. A schema-constrained LLM compiler grounds test intents using roadway, V2X, fault, and driver-behavior knowledge, while an analytic Age-of-Information verifier rejects temporally ineffective scenarios before simulation. During simulation, Signal Phase and Timing (SPaT), MAP, and Basic Safety Message (BSM) messages are transmitted through a spatial communication model, where delays, losses, timestamp errors, and incorrect message content can be introduced. To determine whether communication is truly responsible for a safety failure, LAST-V2X runs two matched scenarios under the same physical traffic conditions: one ego vehicle uses degraded V2X information, while the other receives the corresponding non-degraded information. A failure is accepted only when the degraded information leads to a worse safety outcome. The resulting simulation evidence is then used to refine ineffective scenarios through parameter search and LLM self-reflection, after which successful failure cases are simplified into minimal reproducible scenarios.

Experiments were conducted across eight CARLA towns using a 100-scene natural-language benchmark. Under healthy communication, LAST-V2X produces no spurious communication-induced failures. Under communication faults, it identifies three distinct failure mechanisms: stale-but-detectable, fresh-but-wrong, and silent-by-absence. A separate 50-scene refinement suite further evaluates the adaptive refinement process. LAST-V2X increases the number of causally verified failures from 7 after the initial execution to 32 after refinement, and each successful case is reduced to a minimal single-fault scenario.

Key Ideas

Coupled physical–information generation

LAST-V2X jointly constructs the physical traffic scene and the V2X information the vehicle perceives, with feasibility checks that reject ineffective tests before simulation.

Causal verification

Matched degraded and non-degraded executions decide whether a V2X fault actually changes the physical safety outcome. A failure counts only if the message-driven ego fails and its ground-truth twin does not.

Execution-grounded refinement

Parameter search corrects quantitative deficiencies, LLM self-reflection revises semantically ineffective intents, and delta debugging reduces each success to a minimal single-fault scenario.

Three communication-induced failure mechanisms

  • Stale-but-detectable. Loss or delay raises the Age of Information, which the application can detect, often triggering a safe fallback.
  • Fresh-but-wrong. Messages still look current but carry incorrect content (frozen SPaT, timestamp skew, wrong lane-to-signal mapping), so the vehicle trusts and acts on a wrong traffic state.
  • Silent-by-absence. Safety-critical information never arrives, for example when a conflicting vehicle does not transmit BSMs at all.

Results

Scenario generation at scale

The 100 benchmark prompts describe only the traffic scene and environment. The parser infers the V2X application, and retrieval finds feasible towns. All 100 scenes compile within two parse attempts and run to completion. In the realized scenes, 365 of 367 spawns are clean, all 2,048 checked lane markings match the OpenDRIVE map, and 253 of 254 vehicles stay on route. Under healthy communication the benchmark yields zero admitted communication-induced failures.

Corridor traversal along an RSU chain

One prompt describes a four-junction corridor, and only its information clause changes between runs: no V2X, direct SPaT, or an RSU relay chain. At a 50 m radio range, the relay chain raises the mean number of junctions known ahead from 0.5 to 2.6. Compared with no V2X, GLOSA cuts stops from 1.9 to 1.0 and mean acceleration from 1.70 to 1.21 m/s2, matching direct SPaT at three times the radio range. Across 420 CARLA runs there are no red-light entries and no collisions.

Cooperative scenarios with multiple connected vehicles

In an EEBL relay chain, a V2V rebroadcast lets the third vehicle brake 1.49 s after the lead with no collisions in 10 seeds. Without the relay, 7 of 10 runs end in a rear-end collision. In IMA mutual yield, two equipped crossing vehicles interleave cleanly in 10 of 10 seeds. With one or neither equipped, the encounter becomes a near miss with contact in 2 of 10 seeds: cooperation is pairwise.

Scenario refinement at scale

On a 50-scene fault suite that includes deliberately weak, mistimed, and ineffective cases, the initial execution admits only 7 scenarios. Specification-space search finds failures in 23 of the 43 remaining targets, with a median of 8 executions to the first admission. LLM self-reflection rescues 8, two of them beyond the reach of search. Together the two loops raise admitted failures from 7 to 32, and each is reduced to a minimal single-fault witness.

In rlvw_late_freeze, the original SPaT freeze begins after the ego has already decided to stop, so the run stays clean. From the execution evidence, the LLM identifies the mistimed fault and moves the freeze into the decision window. After the revision, the message-driven ego enters on red while its matched non-degraded twin stops, which is a causally verified failure.
